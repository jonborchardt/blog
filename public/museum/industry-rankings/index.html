tRatio
The correlation coefficient measures how many sums of squares in the shape of the target vector, are matched by the media vector. The critical benefit of this metric is that it is UNIVERAL, GLOBAL, and COMPARABLE between advertisers, industries, and other factors. Under this scheme, a +0.5 in one industry means a certain amount of sums of squares accounted for between the target and media vector. In another industry, a +0.5 may be achieved with different variable values, but means the same thing in terms of how good is the match. It does not need to take into account the number of buyers, which vary from one product to another, and vary in terms of the potential universe of buyers, or the brand’s effectiveness in advertising to-date.

The tRatio is an absolute scale, normalized number from -1..1. Three cases are of special interest:
  1. +1 indicates that the media being purchased is a perfect match for the target.
  2. -1 indicates that the media being purchased is the opposite of what the advertiser should be purchasing. For example, say that the advertiser’s product is “senior life insurance”, which is life insurance that seniors purchase late in life to defray burial costs. A -1 could occur if the advertiser is targeting Cartoon network or MTV which are viewed by very young people, when in fact they should be targeting elderly people.
  3. 0 Indicates that the media being purchased is effectively random. Because of the normalization regime, 0 means that the vector is effectively a match for the average of US population.
Because of the universality of the metric, we can use it to report on overall TV targetedness, and compare different industries to see how their natural levels of targeting vary.

CPM
Cost per thousand impressions.

CPM30
Different advertisers use different lengths of advertisements. Some advertisers use 15 second, 30 second, 60 second and 120 second advertisements. We have found that generally the cost of these ads scales linearly with the number of ad seconds. In order to produce a measurement of CPMs, we have to standardize to a particular media length. We do that by setting 30 second ads as our standard. CPM30 = CPM / (medialength/30). Every airing in the United States is tracked, and an estimate of its clearing price is made. That estimate is often referred to as the “SQAD rate”. We surface these SQAD rates, along with Nielsen quoted impressions, for every airing. This gives us the Cost and Impressions. Finally, we then apply our targeting measure of buyers per million.

  1. How good is your CPM? What CPM has your agency been quoting to you? Check your CPM against the SQAD CPM rate that the industry is getting on average.
