### Progression grammar (`TOKEN` in `lib/harmony.mjs`)

`^([b#]?)([ivIV]+)(m|dim|M(?!7))?(7|M7)?(?:\/([0-9]))?(?:@(\d+))?$` plus `[..]` groups. Rules, from the code comments and tests:

- Case never changes a plain diatonic triad (`V` in C:minor is Gm). Case sets the triad under a seventh (`V7` = dominant seventh in any key; `v7` = the diatonic seventh) and for altered roots (`bVI`).
- `m`/`M`/`dim` force quality (`IVm` = Fm in C:major). `M7` is a major-seventh chord whatever the case (`ivM7` in C:minor = Fmaj7).
- `@n` holds a chord for n bars, 1..64; not allowed inside `[..]`. It expands to one chord per bar underneath, so bass, pad and the check see nothing new.
- `/1` `/2` invert: rotate the lowest tone up an octave; the name gains a slash bass spelled the key's own way (`VI/1` in C:minor = Ab/C).
- `follow: 'tones'` on a melody maps the written degrees onto the current chord's tones (0 root, 1 third, 2 fifth, 3 seventh, 4 root above) instead of the key's scale.
- The Mix card's per-bar chord picker refuses a progression containing `@n` (`index.html`: status `@n is hand-written: edit the progression in the source`; `web/hll-schema.mjs` explains why: the picker's token list is the expanded per-bar cycles, so one `@n` token would occupy n slots).

Verified chord names (verbatim today):

```
C:minor           i iv V i           → Cm Fm Gm Cm        (bare V is not a dominant)
C:harmonic minor  i iv v i           → Cm Fm G Cm         (the scale supplies it)
C:harmonic minor  i iv V7 i          → Cm Fm G7 Cm
C:minor           V7 v7 VM7 ivM7     → G7 Gm7 Gmaj7 Fmaj7
C:major           vii7 ii7 bVII IV7 IVm → Bm7b5 Dm7 Bb F7 Fm
C:minor           i bVI [III VII] V7 → Cm G [Eb Bb] G7    (bVI in C minor: Ab is diatonic; b lowers it to G major... see caveat)
C:minor           i@2 VI/1 V7/2      → Cm Cm Ab/C G7/D
C:minor           i #ivdim V         → Cm F#dim Gm
```

Caveat on `bVI` in C:minor: the accidental is applied to the diatonic degree (Ab → G), which is what the code does and the test pins (`chordNames('C:minor', 'i bVI [III VII] V7')` → `Cm G [Eb Bb] G7`); a musician expecting "bVI = Ab in minor" will be surprised. Do not use `bVI` in minor as an example without saying so; use `bVII` in C:major → Bb, which reads as expected.

Errors are thrown, never defaulted: `bad numeral "dim" in progression ...`, `inversion /3 ... only /1 and /2`, `bars after @ must be 1..64`, `bars (@n) inside [..] must be 1`, `unclosed [`, `relative key is only defined from major or minor, not "dorian"`.

Prefix `very ` when `|v - 0.5| >= 0.35`. Mood words are never searched. Signals and strings are skipped.

## 4. The story for a product-review reader

**What the product is, in one sentence:** a fixed English vocabulary that edits a song's source file by moving numbers, reads the numbers back as English, and tells you exactly how much of what you asked for it managed.

**Strongest beats, in order:**

1. **Deltas, not states, and the honest saturation report.** Say `much darker` twice: the first lands, the second prints `applied +0 of requested -0.60   saturated` on four of five parts. This is unchanged, re-run today, and audible in the existing clips. It is the headline.
2. **The whole delta table is 73 numbers**, in a 29 × 12 grid, and the 25 original vectors have never been retuned; the table was extended once (four depth words) for a new job. Sparse on purpose.
3. **The read-back is lossy and the loss is measured**: 19 of 29 words can be spoken back, 0 of 8 moods, 5 of 24 directions have no word, `hypnotic` applies and reads back as nothing, and two states that differ on four parts read back identically.
4. **Three kinds of word, three kinds of semantics, one sentence.** Control/mood words are deltas (not idempotent, modifiers scale them, `less` flips them). Harmony words are states (modifiers ignored, idempotent). Motion words are shapes (they replace a number with a movement centred on it, and the phrase's own delta is applied first). `much darker, wobbling brightness` does all three things in order and the report shows each. That is the new material and the most reviewable design decision.
5. **Verbs are macros over the same machinery**: `breakdown` is literally the phrase `sparser, more spacious, darker` plus "drop the fx part"; `strip` is a keep-list. Four of them, in JSON, no new mechanism. A reviewer can read `verbs.json` in ten seconds.
6. **Refusals name the reason and the fix**, and the list grew with the vocabulary (a movement on a structural axis, a second movement on the same axis). The spread story changed: the CLI now evaluates the song before editing, so both callers see the baseline. The principle (no baseline, no edit) is intact; the CLI/page contrast is gone.
7. **Taste as a linter, now in two layers**: the song-table lint (rhythm and structure, plus two load rules with a stated hardware ceiling of ~40 voices) and the mix lint over a rendered measurement (headroom, inaudible parts, flat stage, masking, depth contrast). The song lint still cannot see brightness: darker2 lints identically to demo. The mix lint can, but it needs a render.

**What is surprising:**

- `pulse` sits at the top and dips; `swell` peaks at the section's midpoint, not a quarter in (the generated doc is wrong, the code is right).
- Four of the ten mute words are the four newest, because they were designed to move three axes gently and so never own a column. The depth words are invisible to the read-back by construction.
- The listed songs still total exactly 29 warnings, 15 of them from `machine`, six days and four new songs later: every new song (`breakwater`, `cutwork`, `foundry`, `glassworks`) passes clean.
- `bVI` in a minor key lowers the diatonic Ab to G. Musically odd, tested, documented nowhere in prose.
