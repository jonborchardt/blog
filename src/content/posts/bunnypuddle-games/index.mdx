Not everything in the portfolio is adtech. Around 2010 — the same year I moved from Bing to
PrecisionDemand — I published a couple of Android games under the name BunnyPuddle Games. The
developer link is still sitting in the header of
[the portfolio site](/labs-2018-snapshot/), next to LinkedIn and GitHub, wedged in with everything
else I'd shipped.

## Zen Puddle

The one I remember best is a water simulation. You dragged your finger across the screen and it
rippled like real water — refraction, reflection, a slider to trade quality for performance, your
choice of ten backgrounds to run the water over.

<Figure caption="Zen Puddle on the Play Store: ten backgrounds, a detail slider, simulated refraction and reflection." width="wide">
  <img src={zp.src} width={zp.width} height={zp.height} alt="Zen Puddle Play Store listing with background texture thumbnails for tile, brick, and stone" />
</Figure>

There was no product reason for it. I built it because getting water to look and move like water
is a genuinely fun problem, and nobody was asking me to solve it.

## Zen Tunnel

The other one shipped under the same name and isn't about water at all — a first-person tunnel
flight where you tilt the phone to steer and tap to shoot down the robots chasing you. Same
studio name, same "because it sounded fun" energy, different itch.

<Figure caption="Zen Tunnel on the Play Store: tilt to steer through the tunnel, tap to shoot down the robots chasing you." width="wide">
  <img src={zt.src} width={zt.width} height={zt.height} alt="Zen Tunnel Play Store listing showing a dark tiled tunnel with glowing green obstacles" />
</Figure>

Both games shipped. I don't remember much beyond that — no launch story, no download numbers I
still have access to, no grand plan. It was 2010, I wanted to make something with water in it, and
then I wanted to make something with a tunnel in it, so I did.
