The last project in this portfolio, chronologically, is also the smallest gallery in it: one HTML
page, a video, and two screenshots. It is the ONE TV platform again — the one this series has been
circling — but pointed at Australia instead of the United States, for **MCN, the Multi Channel
Network**, an Australian TV ad-sales house. Same product, same UI skin, a different country's
ratings system underneath it.

## What shipped

I led the UI team that built this during my last stretch at AOL, after it became Oath. MCN's
deployment did go out the door — it launched. I don't have a rollout story or a launch-day anecdote
to attach to it; what I have is my own answer to the one question that matters here, which is
whether the thing actually worked: it did, and it worked the way the US platform worked, but the
hard parts were different — **currency, time zones, and rating numbers**.

"Currency" in TV ad sales is not money, it is the unit you buy and report against. The US platform
ran on Nielsen impressions. Australia runs on **OzTAM**, the country's television ratings body, and
the unit its buyers think in is **TARPs** — target audience rating points — not raw impressions.
Re-platforming onto that meant the whole forecasting and pacing pipeline had to speak a different
number for the same idea of "how much audience did this schedule reach."

## The UI gives it away before the copy does

Nothing in the screenshots announces "this is the Australian build." What gives it away is
smaller: the dates read DD/MM, not MM/DD; the inventory source field says "MCN National
Subscription TV UAT"; and the forecast panel has TARPs columns sitting right next to the impression
columns it inherited from the US version.

The community groups in that distribution table are worth pausing on: Factual, Music, General,
Movies, Lifestyle, Sport. That is not the US platform's category list re-skinned — it is a
different content taxonomy entirely, because Australian subscription TV inventory is organized
differently than what the US version was built to categorize. Getting a forecast right meant
mapping into that taxonomy, not just relabeling one that already existed.

The pacing report tells the same story from the delivery side, once a campaign is actually
running rather than being planned:

by week — just built on OzTAM's numbers instead of Nielsen's, and dated the way Australia dates
things.

## What I don't have

I don't have a clean one-paragraph description of what ONE TV was as a whole product, or how the
upfront and scatter sides of it split the work — I've been honest throughout this series about
where my memory has gaps, and that one is real. What I do have, for this project specifically, is
enough: it launched, it worked the way the US version worked, and the difference was currency,
time zones, and rating numbers. That's a smaller claim than a launch narrative, but it's the one I
can actually stand behind.

## Where this leaves things, in 2026

This is the last project in the portfolio and the last post in this series. The thing I want to
leave you with is not really about MCN — it's about the folder all twelve of these posts came
from. In 2026, the entire portfolio [I wrote about reopening](/labs-2018-snapshot/), MCN included,
came back to life with one command, because none of it was ever more than static files. Nothing in
here needed a server to keep running. It just needed someone to point a directory listing at it
again.
