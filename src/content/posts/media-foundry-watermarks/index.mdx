Buying a television spot and having it actually air are two different events, and PrecisionDemand's
whole pitch — tell an advertiser what market their ad dollars would convert into real sales — falls
apart if the second event doesn't happen. In 2013 I built the tool that checked whether it had:
Media Foundry.

## The problem: proof, not just a plan

A media plan is a promise: this spot, on this station, in this week. Whether the station kept that
promise is a separate question, and the only way to answer it is to watch television and see what
actually ran. Media Foundry didn't do that watching itself — we paid outside companies to watch for
an ad watermark embedded in the spot, all over the country, and report back every time and place
they saw it. Media Foundry's job was to take that stream of detections and reconcile it against the
plan.

Each column is a planned spot block — a time window, the days of the week it runs, and how many
spots the plan called for and at what price. Each row is a detection: a timestamp and a spot ID that
some outside spotter's watermark scan caught airing. The circles between them mark, block by block,
whether that detection lines up with a block in the plan. The far right column, "Detected but not in
plan," catches everything that aired without a plan to explain it.

## Reconciling plan against reality

Strip away the grid chrome and three things can happen to any given block or detection: a detection
matches a planned block, a planned block never gets a matching detection, or a detection shows up
with nowhere in the plan to put it.

That third case — a detection with nowhere to go — is where the grid stopped being read-only. Drag
a row out of "Detected but not in plan" and drop it onto a block, and Media Foundry created a new
airing record on the spot: a way to correct the reconciliation by hand instead of leaving a mismatch
sitting on the report. It's a small affordance, but it's the one that turns the grid from a status
display into a tool someone actually used to close out a week.

## Sole developer, one week at a time

Media Foundry was PrecisionDemand-era work, built in 2013 while I was the company's sole UI developer
— every screen in the product, this one included, was mine to design and build. Reconciliation tools
are not glamorous; they exist because a claim ("your ad ran") is worth nothing without a way to check
it, week after week, campaign after campaign. This was PrecisionDemand's way of checking.
